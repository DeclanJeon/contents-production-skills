# MBTI lens: four preference axes as creative questions

Read when `typology_mode` is `mbti` or `both`. The four pairs below are this repo's own paraphrase of the official axis descriptions [1]; they are writing questions about how a character gathers information, organizes thought, decides, and adjusts plans — not a personality inventory, test items, or scoring.

## The four axes as questions

| Axis | Creative question | Observable territory |
|---|---|---|
| E–I | Where does this character's energy and attention point — outward to people/action or inward to reflection? | Who they seek out under stress, whether they think aloud or alone, how they recharge between scenes |
| S–N | What information do they trust first — concrete, verified, present-tense detail or pattern, implication, possibility? | What they check before acting, whether they quote specifics or extrapolate, what they dismiss as noise |
| T–F | What does their decision procedure optimize — consistency and impersonal criteria, or impact on people and values? | How they justify a choice, what they consider a fair outcome, what makes a decision feel wrong |
| J–P | How do they handle the outer world's demands — closing and structuring early, or staying open and adapting? | Their relationship to plans, deadlines, and surprises; what "done" means to them |

## Interpretation rules

- Record the MBTI lens's character-specific **design rationale** and **limits** in `typology_lens`, including MBTI-only packets. For each axis: selected pole or undecided `position`, its grounds and `supplied | observed | proposal | undecided` provenance, **character-specific interpretation** (not a generic pole description), **connecting behavior**, and an **exception condition**. Rationale is a creative choice/evidence locator, never a verified test result; limits include missing inputs and unsupported inferences.
- Preference ≠ ability: an introvert-coded character can still command a room; the axis describes what costs more, not what is impossible. Likewise T can feel deeply and F can reason sharply — the axis describes which justification they reach for first.
- A type string (e.g. ISTJ) is a summary, not a spec. Expand it through the persona core — this character's motives, values, and conditions — never copy a stock "type portrait."
- Strength labels (low/mid/high) are relative creative markers only — never percentages, test scores, or statistical claims.
- Cognitive-function stacks, subtypes, and clinical framing are out of the default path.

## Misuse boundaries

- Never infer a real person's type from appearance, speech, or conduct; never type an existing approved character without delegation; never treat a supplied type as a verified assessment — record it as supplied fact.
- No axis maps to face, build, gender, ethnicity, voice pitch, or timbre. E is not loudness; I is not low pitch or weak social skill.
- Same type, different person: two ISTJs can protect different things — keep motive, priority, and exception conditions distinct.
- If supplied behavior contradicts the type interpretation, the behavior wins; record the gap rather than bending either.

## Evidence status

The official description presents the pairs as preferences, not abilities, and allows acting outside preference [1]. A 1989 study of 468 adults (McCrae & Costa) did not support discrete types or true dichotomies, treating the indicators as relatively independent dimensions, while reporting correlations with some personality dimensions — read as scope-limited, older-instrument evidence, not a verdict on every MBTI use [11]. This skill uses the axes as design questions and makes no empirical claim. See `sources.md` for citation scope.

Read scope: [1] official MBTI Facts questions 5–7 and 14 (`READ-PARTIAL`, 2026-10-16); [11] PubMed abstract (`READ-PARTIAL`, same date). The observable-territory column is an authored set of writing prompts, not a finding that those behaviors establish a person's preference. In particular, the S–N axis is about preferred information, not a claim that one pole verifies truth and the other does not.
