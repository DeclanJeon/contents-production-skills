# FrameAtlas · Visual Foundations

Original public-use field guide · CC0-1.0

## Thesis

Write one sentence about what this world asks the audience to feel. Use it as a test for every later choice.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Internal logic

Choose three rules that remain true across every image, scene and object. Consistency lets unusual ideas feel believable.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Archetypes

Define the recurring people, places and objects through their roles, motives and visible habits.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Visual language

Choose a restrained palette, a material family and a lighting rule. Describe the decisions rather than naming a vague style.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Composition

Decide how space expresses status, distance and belonging. Repeat a framing principle with purposeful variations.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Tone

Name the emotional temperature and the details that would break it. A cheerful prop can change a solemn scene.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Atmosphere

Use weather, air and ambient sound to connect locations. Keep atmosphere consistent with geography and time.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Scale

Define the relationship between a person and their environment. Show one familiar object to make unfamiliar scale readable.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Story

Give the central figure a want, an obstacle and a visible choice. The choice should change something we can see.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Pacing

Allocate time according to importance. Let a discovery settle before rushing into the next image.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Editing

Connect shots through direction, light, action or contrast. Establish enough geography that a change in angle makes sense.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Repetition and variation

Repeat an object or composition with one changed detail. Let the difference tell us that time or meaning has shifted.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.

## Signature

Choose one deliberate recurring decision that belongs to this project. Use it sparingly enough to remain recognizable.

Exercise: Name one visible detail in your scene that demonstrates this principle.
