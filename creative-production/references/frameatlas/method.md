# FrameAtlas · The Field Method

Original public-use field guide · CC0-1.0

## 01. A useful beginning

Choose a small scene and a clear emotional purpose.

Write a one-line premise, select a setting you understand and choose one observable action. Keep the first exercise small enough to finish in an afternoon.

Exercise: Write a premise.

## 02. Think in coherent worlds

Turn isolated images into a connected visual idea.

Choose three rules for the scene: material, light and behavior. Explain one detail you will reject because it would break those rules.

Exercise: Make a rule card.

## 03. A practical shot language

Match camera position to the information a viewer needs.

Try a wide establishing view, a medium action and a close detail. Keep the subject, location, direction of movement and lighting consistent across all three.

Exercise: Plan three frames.

## 04. Light with intention

Choose a source and let its direction shape the scene.

Begin with one window or visible lamp. Decide which side of the subject it lights and how much detail belongs in shadow. Add another source only if the story needs it.

Exercise: Describe one source.

## 05. Build a short sequence

Use action and reaction to make a scene readable.

Make four connected frames: the place, the action, the crucial detail and the consequence. Each frame should contribute information that the others cannot.

Exercise: Build four beats.

## 06. Refine and finish

Remove contradictions and preserve your useful discoveries.

Review screen direction, eye lines, light direction, recurring objects and subject identity. Save the prompts beside the final choices so you can reproduce your decisions.

Exercise: Run a continuity pass.

## 07. A repeatable field kit

Keep a compact set of tools for the next project.

Save your premise, rules, shot list and rejected examples in one small document. Reuse the questions on a new subject while letting the answers change.

Exercise: Save your field kit.
