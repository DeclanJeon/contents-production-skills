# Voice, lip-sync, and localization research memo

## Actionable guidance grounded in primary sources

- **Separate the localization stages:** transcribe/identify speakers → translate and adapt meaning/register → fit each target line and pause to the scene → synthesize target-language speech → align captions to the *rendered* speech → lip-sync against that final audio → mix and inspect. This is a practical synthesis of the capabilities/limitations described by the dubbing papers and vendor APIs, not a guarantee of any particular pipeline’s output.
- **Treat duration as an editorial constraint, not a word-count conversion.** Translation changes spoken length. Preserve meaning and natural prosody first, then decide which wording, pause, edit, or supported timing control resolves a mismatch. Don’t speed-adjust or truncate blindly. The human-localization corpus study cautions against treating strict character/length matching or lip-sync as absolute priorities; resolve tradeoffs for the scene and audience.
- **Pin a voice and verify by listening.** Record the actual voice/model/version/settings, target language/accent evidence and rights/consent basis; reuse the selected identity across scenes, but don’t infer perceived consistency from a reused ID or a provider’s language list. Listen to full lines, risky names, cuts between lines, and final mixed context. Current vendor docs describe controls/capabilities, not proof of a chosen voice’s performance on specific copy.
- **Keep performance direction distinct from identity.** Preserve stable voice/accent anchors while varying delivery for scene intent. Only use controls confirmed for the selected model/version; descriptive prompts and tags are not deterministic control guarantees.
- **Lip-sync is downstream of final audio.** First lock the spoken wording and audio timing, then synchronize mouth motion to that target-language audio. A lip-sync model’s audio-to-mouth capability does not itself translate dialogue, preserve a voice, adapt line duration, or guarantee natural acting. Review prominent closures/stressed syllables, shot boundaries and listening reactions, not just continuous mouth motion.
- **Caption timing belongs to the final rendered track.** Word/segment alignment from source audio is not automatically alignment for a newly generated translation. Use generated-audio alignment when available, then review timing and reading/line breaks in the actual target format/player. Distinguish technical cue validity from human readability/accessibility.
- **Synchronize against a stable media timeline.** Keep source and output time bases/frame rate explicit; use actual measured cue/audio/video offsets, not text length or guessed durations. Re-check dependent captions/lip-sync after audio changes. Test playback and transition boundaries; retain separate audio-only, silent-video and full-playback checks when relevant.

## Primary sources and claim scope

### Standards
- **W3C, WebVTT: The Web Video Text Tracks Format**, current published page identified in search as Candidate Recommendation Draft, 20 May 2026: https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/ . Defines cue start/end timestamps, ordering and overlap rules, timestamp syntax and inline timestamps. Scope: WebVTT format semantics; it does not prescribe translation quality, readable caption length, or prove AV sync in a rendered player.

### Official product/API documentation (rolling docs; no fixed publication/version date stated on the cited pages; consulted 2026-10-13)
- **ElevenLabs TTS guide:** https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/playground/text-to-speech . Documents voice/model/settings and language-related guidance; capability guidance, not empirical validation of a particular generated line. Their documentation recommends matching voice language/accent to target language; confirm the chosen voice against actual copy.
- **ElevenLabs Dubbing:** https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/dubbing . Describes dubbing workflow/capabilities including speaker handling and similarity/naturalness tradeoff. Does not establish guaranteed voice identity or translation quality.
- **Bring your own transcript (ElevenLabs):** https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-api/guides/how-to/dubbing/bring-your-own-transcript . Explains transcript-segment timing inputs and warns that translated spoken-length differences affect pacing; useful evidence that timing needs deliberate handling, not a universal duration rule.
- **Forced Alignment (ElevenLabs):** https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/forced-alignment . Alignment of supplied text with supplied audio; use only for that audio/text pair. Alignment is not translation and does not align a different subsequently generated track.
- **ElevenLabs TTS with timestamps endpoint:** https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-dialogue/convert-with-timestamps . API capability for generated dialogue and timestamps; not an independent guarantee those timestamps satisfy a subtitle format/player’s reading or accessibility needs.
- **OpenAI text-to-speech guide:** https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/text-to-speech . Describes speech generation, voice/instructions, supported languages and limitations; API documentation only, not measured output quality. Consult current model-specific docs at execution time.
- **Google Cloud SSML:** https://docs.cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/docs/ssml . Describes SSML and supported marks/timepoints where applicable. Mark timepoints are explicit marked locations, not automatic word-level alignment for arbitrary translated output.
- **Google Cloud Translation docs:** https://docs.cloud.google.com/translate/docs . Text-translation API scope; it is a building block, not a full dialogue adaptation/dubbing/lip-sync workflow.

### Peer-reviewed primary methods/evaluations
- **Prajwal et al., “A Lip Sync Expert Is All You Need for Speech to Lip Generation In The Wild,” ACM Multimedia 2020**, paper: https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/files/211225619/Wav2Lip_ACMMM_20_camera_ready_2_.pdf . Primary Wav2Lip method. Scope is visual speech/lip synchronization from audio; not dialogue translation, voice cloning, or localization duration adaptation.
- **Yang et al., “Large-scale multilingual audio visual dubbing,” 2020 preprint:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03530 . Describes an integrated research pipeline for translation, target-language speech, duration handling and mouth-frame generation. Its training languages/data/assumptions are paper-specific; do not generalize that coverage to current commercial APIs or all languages.
- **Behr et al., “Face-Dubbing++: Lip-Synchronous, Voice Preserving Translation of Videos,” 2022:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04523 . Research system integrating ASR, MT, prosody-controlled TTS, voice conversion and lip generation; supports treating these as coupled but distinct stages. Paper results/limitations are scoped to that method and evaluation.
- **Brannon et al., “Dubbing in Practice: A Large Scale Study of Human Localization With Insights for Automatic Dubbing,” TACL 2023:** https://transacl.org/index.php/tacl/article/view/4447 . Analysis of professional localization corpus; supports nuanced treatment of timing constraints relative to translation and vocal naturalness. It is corpus evidence, not a universal acceptance rule or a model benchmark.

- **Speech synthesis / voice consistency:** Strongly covered in 22: versioned voice identity, separation of identity direction and performance cue, supported-control checks, rights/consent, actual full-result listening, risk-word review, voice/mix QA, bounded retries, and stale downstream assets when audio changes. Existing advice explicitly avoids promising consistency from ID/settings alone.
- **Lip-sync / sync:** Strongly covered in 22 and 09: generate/lock audio before precision lip-sync; mark prominent syllables/closures; distinguish estimates from measurements; review audio-mouth offset and listeners; record measured sync anchors/offsets; do not infer timing from copy or ASR. 23 covers finishing and playback review.
- **Captions and delivery:** Strongly covered in 15: synchronize to final audio, source/translation comparison, provisional timing vs measured timing, start/end/order/overlap checks and rendered review; audio changes stale dependent captions/lip-sync. 09 warns against using ASR as timing proof.
- **Localization:** Partial. 15 asks to compare translated captions for names, units, proper nouns, speakers and meaning, while 22 checks selected voice language/accent and risky pronunciations. `marketing-source-adaptation.md` covers translated caption overlays and overlap with source hard captions. I found no clear general dialogue-localization procedure for translated line adaptation to speaker intent/register, locale-specific formality/dialect, idioms/cultural references, name pronunciation handoff, or per-line duration/shot-window review before TTS.

## Concrete skill gap (limited)

One narrow, actionable gap appears to be an **explicit multilingual dialogue localization/adaptation pass**, not a new voice/lip-sync or caption QA framework. If later authorized for skill changes, add a small step in the existing dialogue owner (22) or screenplay/delivery handoff requiring each localized line to retain source meaning and character intent, record locale/register and approved names/pronunciation, compare spoken duration against the actual turn/shot window after draft translation, and flag unresolved semantic-vs-timing tradeoffs for review before synthesis. Keep the downstream voice-selection, measured-timing and caption procedures already present; do not duplicate them. I did not identify a concrete gap warranting a separate generic speech-synthesis or AV-sync skill.
